MAIR Mentorship Program
The MAIR Mentorship Program is a selective, end-to-end program designed to equip early-stage Moroccan AI researchers with the skills, network, and ongoing support they need to thrive in their research journey. It is not a one-off event — it is a structured, long-term commitment between MAIR and each cohort.
Who is it for?
The program is open to Master's students planning to pursue a PhD, incoming PhD students who haven't started yet, and early-stage PhD students in their first years. If you're Moroccan, passionate about AI research, and ready to invest in your growth as a researcher, this program is for you.
How does it work?
The program runs in four phases. It starts with a selective application process — for the May 2026 cohort, only 20 spots are available. We look for motivation, clarity of research direction, and willingness to engage with the community long-term.
Once selected, you join the PhD Research Kickstarter, an intensive one-week bootcamp where experienced Moroccan AI researchers walk you through everything your PhD program won't teach you: research tools and workflows, the publishing ecosystem, how to write and structure papers, how to communicate your work, and how to build the right mindset for the long road ahead.
But the bootcamp is just the entry point. After the week ends, the real program begins. Each cohort gets a dedicated private group within the MAIR community server — a space to share resources, discuss papers, ask questions, and support each other throughout the PhD. You also get paired with a mentor for regular one-on-one sessions tailored to your specific challenges and goals. On top of that, monthly group check-ins bring the entire cohort together so you can learn from each other's progress, setbacks, and breakthroughs.
Why does it matter?
Doing a PhD can be isolating, especially for Moroccan researchers who often lack access to structured mentorship, peer networks, or guidance on navigating the global research landscape. The MAIR Mentorship Program exists to close that gap — not with a single workshop, but with sustained, community-driven support that lasts as long as your research journey does.
